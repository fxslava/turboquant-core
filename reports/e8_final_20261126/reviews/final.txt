Admission blocker WATCHDOG-ORPHAN
- run_canary.sh applies timeout --signal=TERM --kill-after=15s 900s to the Python verifier. The verifier starts the native encoder with subprocess.Popen and has no SIGTERM handler. Its except BaseException cleanup does not run when the OS terminates Python with its default SIGTERM action.
- Default subprocess.Popen closes the inherited phase guard and exclusive lane descriptors in the encoder child. When GNU timeout sees its direct Python child exit on TERM, timeout can return 124 before grace ends. Its pending KILL is discarded, the outer lock holders return, and a TERM-resistant encoder can still be active as another build or simulator acquires the corresponding locks.
- An isolated CPU negative control ran the exact sealed run_verified_benchmark.py under temporary exclusive flock with the same timeout/Python topology, shortening the watchdog to 2s and the kill grace to 1s. The child was Python, mapped a mock PEM that the verifier verified, and ignored SIGTERM. The wrapper exited 124 after 2.03s, the child was sleeping after the grace elapsed, and the temporary exclusive lane was immediately reacquirable. The control explicitly killed the CPU child. An independent minimal Popen topology reproduced the same outcome.
- Attach the watchdog directly to the native process inside the verifier, and preserve /proc verification of the live native process identity. Alternatively, ensure the verifier handles TERM and terminates, kills and reaps its child before it returns to the lock holders. Repeat the resistant-child control and reseal the launch identity before readmission. Technical defect sent directly to /root/kernel_worker.
